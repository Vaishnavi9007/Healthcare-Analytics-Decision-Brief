Executive summary

This package demonstrates how patient cohort, pharmacy dispensing, and safety-report summaries could inform an operational review workflow. The current example is synthetic; it supports no real-world clinical or operational conclusion. The defensible next step is to validate definitions and data quality, then measure workflow performance under clinical and privacy oversight.

Operational question and measures

Question: Can a weekly medication-review queue help pharmacy operations identify records that merit review, and improve timely review without creating inequitable or privacy-invasive workflows?

  • Data completeness for age band, medication, fill date, and review status.
  • Share of eligible queue records reviewed within seven days.
  • Actionable-review rate, adjudicated by an authorized clinical team.
  • Refill-gap trend against a defined baseline and comparison period.
  • Coverage, missingness, and performance by relevant subgroups.
Recommendation: Authorize a governed data-validation and pilot-design phase. Do not use the demo numbers to identify, rank, or intervene on real patients.

Evidence and interpretation

The dashboard joins three aggregate demonstration tables: age-band/risk-flag counts, pharmacy fill/refill-gap counts, and safety-report/seriousness counts. In the assigned-data version, document source, date range, extraction date, inclusion rules, duplicate handling, field mapping, and every denominator. Treat cross-dataset joins as exploratory until data owners validate the linkage.

A dispensed fill does not prove the patient took the medication. Spontaneous adverse-event reports may be incomplete, duplicated, unverified, and affected by reporting behavior. Serious-report proportions describe reports, not patient-level risk. Multiple products and events within a report do not establish a causal pair.

Privacy, bias, and quality

  • Use approved, minimum-necessary, de-identified or aggregated data; restrict access to authorized staff.
  • Suppress small cells according to organizational policy and avoid re-identification through combined filters.
  • Assess missingness, age-group coverage, and representation before comparing groups.
  • Require clinical governance to define review criteria; monitor false positives and workload.
  • Prespecify baseline, cohort, time window, and success thresholds before interpreting pilot results.
